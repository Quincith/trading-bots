# Lab experiment 20261115-copycat-3: Copycat

**❌ Rejected: Also not worse on the study months**

**Idea:** Raise the profit goal from 20% to 30% so the few strong winners can keep running before we sell.

**Why:** This strategy loses about 10% on each bad trade and gains only small amounts on most others. Its whole profit comes from a handful of stocks that jump fast after insiders buy. Selling those at exactly +20% caps our best days, while the losses stay the same size. A higher goal lets the rare big winner make up for more of the losers. Filters on insider count or buy size would have thrown away winners too. This is a guess the data cannot prove, because we don't know how high those 5 stocks went after we sold.

**What Claude noticed:** The losses are not tied to any one signal column. Losers and winners look alike on insider count, score, dollar size and chase. Single-insider trades won 6 of 11, versus 11 of 32 with 2+ insiders. Trades scoring 5+ won only 6 of 16. Buys under $250k won 2 of 6, but those 2 wins outweighed the 4 losses. What drives the result is the exits. 13 stop-outs averaged -9.62% (about -125 points in total). 25 time-limit trades averaged only +1.53% (about +38). Just 5 trades reached the +20% target (about +100). Those 5 alone made up most of the gains. They got there fast: MKZR in 5 days, SEI in 6, GME in 14, VAC and CDTX in 17. The profit cap cuts off the only trades that pay for the losers.

**Change:**

- `copycat.target_pct`: 20 → 30 (Profit goal this % above our entry)
